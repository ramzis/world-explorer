Made for phones: gestures, drone sticks and a landscape mode

World Explorer switches to a touch interface automatically on phones and tablets, and it has two ways to fly, so you can pick the one that feels natural. You change modes with the button in the top-left stack, and your choice carries into landscape.

Gesture mode (the default)

Think Google Earth: one finger pans the map, pinch zooms (altitude), a two-finger vertical swipe tilts the camera and a two-finger twist rotates the view. Movement keeps a little momentum when you let go, a double-tap glides you to the spot you tapped, and the North button snaps back to a top-down, north-up view.

Drone sticks

Tap Sticks and two virtual joysticks appear: the left stick moves you forward and sideways, the right stick looks around (turn and tilt), and the up and down arrow buttons climb and descend, like an RC drone controller. The stick speed is proportional to how far you push.

Landscape: the immersive view

Rotate your phone and the interface steps aside: the buttons hide for a clean full-screen view of the terrain, while the controls you picked stay live. Rotate back to portrait to change mode or reach the menu. It's the best way to take in a mountain range.

8. Worldle and Globle: the daily puzzles

Coffee-break puzzle6 guessesHot or cold
  • WorldleGuess the country from its shape, 6 tries
  • GlobleOne mystery country a day; hotter colour = closer
  • PriceFree; Worldle sells an ad-free Passport
  • TimeA couple of minutes a day

Worldle shows the silhouette of a country (or territory) and gives you six guesses, with clues after every attempt. It has grown into a small suite: practice mode, flashcards, an archive of past puzzles, a challenge mode, leagues and a streak calendar, with a paid Passport that removes the ads.

Globle flips the idea around: every day there is a new Mystery Country, and each incorrect guess appears on a 3D globe in a colour showing how close it is. The hotter the colour, the closer you are. It's a lovely, quiet game that teaches you which countries sit next to which.

Pros

  • Fast daily habit, great for streaks

  • Free to play

Cons

  • One puzzle a day, then wait

Where World Explorer differs: it's a full 10-round exploration game rather than a once-a-day puzzle, so you can play as long as you like.
